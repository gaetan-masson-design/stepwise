# Harmony patterns in electronic music

These are genre conventions that Stepwise uses for presets and suggestions. They describe widely used, generic patterns: chord loops, tempos and song structures are common building blocks of these genres and don't belong to any one artist or work. Nothing here is copied from a specific song.

**How settled each point is:**
- **Established:** a widely documented convention.
- **Working assumption:** our own reading of the genre, good enough to build on.
- **Open:** not settled yet; worth checking by ear.

## Summary
- **Minor keys dominate,** but loops spend much of their time on the major chords VI, III and VII. *(established)*
- **The core vocabulary is natural minor:** i, VI, III, VII, plus iv and v. *(established)*
- **Loops almost always stay in one key.** Colour comes from sus2/sus4, add9, 7ths, and occasionally a Dorian IV or a harmonic-minor V. *(established)*
- **Chords change slowly:**
  - Melodic techno and trance: one chord every 2 bars in an 8-bar loop. *(established)*
  - Pop-leaning dance music: often one chord per bar. *(working assumption)*
- **The less melodic the genre, the fewer chords:** minimal and dub styles often hold one chord. *(established)*
- **Arrangements are built in 8/16-bar blocks,** and the breakdown usually reuses the drop's chords. *(established)*
- **Listeners tend to enjoy moderate harmonic surprise most:** not too predictable, not too complex. *(established)*

## Modes and keys
- **Natural minor (Aeolian)** is the default mode of dance music. *(established)*
- **Dorian** gives a "lifted" minor. *(established)*
- **Harmonic minor** gives drama and a strong pull home. *(established)*
- **Phrygian** gives an "Eastern" colour. *(established)*
- **Common keys:** A, E, F, F♯ and G minor. Keys whose root sits well on a subwoofer are favoured. *(working assumption)*

## How fast chords change, and their colour
- **Melodic techno:** 4 chords over 8 bars. **Trance:** 8 chords over 16 bars. **Pop-dance:** about 1 chord per bar.
- **Bass:**
  - Melodic techno: rolling root notes in 16ths.
  - Progressive: 8th-note chord pulses, with the bass copying the lowest chord note an octave down.
  - A pedal on the home note under changing chords *(working assumption)*.
- **Colours:**
  - sus2 and sus4 (a very common pad sound).
  - add9 and 7ths.
  - Stacked detuned "supersaw" chords across about 3 octaves.
  - Chords without the 3rd, for punch.

## Which chord comes next (minor key; *working assumption*)

| From | Safe | Adventurous | Spicy |
|---|---|---|---|
| i | VI, III, VII | iv, v | IV (Dorian lift), ♭II (Phrygian) |
| VI | III, VII, iv | i | V (harmonic minor), the VI→VII→i run |
| III | VII, VI | iv, i | IIIsus4 |
| VII | VI, i | III | V |
| iv | VI, i, III | v | V or Vsus4 |
| v | III, VI | i | V (raise the 3rd) |
| IV (Dorian) | V, i | III | — |
| V (harmonic) | i, VI | — | I (major home chord, a Picardy ending) |

**Loop rules:**
- Close loops so the last chord leads back to i; VII, VI and v are the usual closers.
- Keep most suggestions in the key, and add surprise sparingly.

## Melody
- **Call-and-response:** a 4-bar call answered from bar 3 onward, forming 8-bar phrases. The answer changes octave, sound or intensity. *(established)*
- **Short motifs** repeated with small variations; arpeggios for movement. *(established)*
- **Melodic-techno leads:** a one-bar figure re-pitched to fit each chord. *(established)*
- **Arpeggios:** usually 16th notes, often doubled an octave up. *(established)*
- **Landing notes:** notes of the current chord are the safe default. Which scale degrees hooks favour is still **open**.

## Structure templates (bars)

| Style | Template |
|---|---|
| Melodic techno | Intro 32 → intro lead 16 → breakdown 24 (vocal enters) → build 16 → drop 32 |
| Melodic house & techno (short) | Intro 16 → build 16 → drop 16 → breakdown 16 → climax 16 |
| Trance | Intro → build → breakdown → build → anthem → outro, in 16-bar blocks; the anthem reuses the breakdown chords |
| Progressive | 4/8/16-bar phrases with long builds |
| Big room | Two builds, two drops, one or two breakdowns |
| Organic song form | Verse and refrain on the same chords; only the bridge moves |
